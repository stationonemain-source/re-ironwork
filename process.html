How a door gets made

Twelve to sixteen weeks from your ticket to a door on its hinges. This is where the time goes.

Measure Week 1 Draw Weeks 2-3 Forge Weeks 4-12 Finish Weeks 12-14 Install Weeks 14-16
Week 1

Measure

We template the opening ourselves, never from a builder's plan. Plumb, square and header height decide more of the design than taste does, and it is cheaper to find out now than at install.

Weeks 2-3

Draw

Your ticket becomes a full-size elevation on paper. You mark it up, we redraw it, and nothing is cut until you sign it. This is where the configurator door gets fitted to your real opening.

Weeks 4-12

Forge

Six to ten weeks at the anvil, depending on the style. Tuscan and Gothic take the longest; Modern and Flush the least. You get photographs at the frame, the ornament and the glazing.

Weeks 12-14

Finish

Every finish is applied by hand and sealed. You approve it on an offcut from your own door, not a chip card, because bronze and patina come out different on every piece.

Weeks 14-16

Install

The same crew that built it hangs it, usually in one or two days. We set the locks, adjust the swing, and come back at ninety days once the house has moved a little.

Questions people ask first

Is the door in the configurator the door I get?
It is the starting point. The photographs show each style, finish and glass together, and the shop builds to a full-size drawing of your opening. Proportions change with the opening; the ironwork, finish and glass do not.
Is forged iron different from what a fabricator sells?
Yes, and you can see it. Forged scrolls taper and thicken where the metal was moved under a hammer. Cold-bent bar is the same thickness all the way round, and cast ornament repeats identically on every door in a catalogue.
What about rust and Oklahoma weather?
Everything is primed and sealed, and exterior doors get a finish specified for west and south exposure. You get a maintenance sheet, the ninety-day visit, and re-oiling at cost after the first year.
Can you restore an old iron door?
Often. Send photographs and a rough measurement and we will say plainly whether it is worth restoring or cheaper to start again.
